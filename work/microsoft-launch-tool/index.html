03 / THE DESIGN RATIONALE

Every view answers
a planner’s question.

The reconstruction centers on a proposed planner job: “When a launch is approaching, help me understand readiness, resolve open decisions and make a credible launch commitment.”

01

Start with the next decision.

A planner needs to see what is blocked, who owns it and what happens next. Readiness, approvals and risks share the first view.

02

Make consumption understandable.

Keep each meter’s quantity, unit, region and pricing assumption together. A usage forecast is distinct from estimated cost and recognized revenue.

03

Bring the outside view inside.

Market briefs, customer feedback and reviews sit beside the launch plan, with their source and relevance visible.

04

Connect design to delivery.

Work items include acceptance criteria, review states and release dependencies so the design can be discussed with developers and tested.

04 / FROM DESIGN TO DEVELOPMENT

Make the handoff
part of the design.

The proposed delivery model makes design intent reviewable: a user need becomes a work item, acceptance criteria, a testable state and a release decision.

One component. Every state.

The approval panel demonstrates pending, approved and changes-requested states. It keeps the plan version, reviewer and evidence together. The same visual language carries through status labels, tables and dialogs.

Awaiting reviewApprovedChanges requested

A reviewable sprint artifact.

In the Delivery view, open a sample work item to inspect its owner, sprint and acceptance criteria. Business-plan approval remains separate from a production deployment approval.

05 / VALIDATION & OUTCOMES

Prove the experience
with the work.

These are proposed validation tasks and measures for the reconstruction. Historical usability findings and performance metrics have not been supplied, so no improvement percentages are claimed.

01

Find the next decision

Ask a planner to locate the highest-priority blocker and its owner.

Measure time, wrong turns and whether the next action is understood.

02

Explain the forecast

Ask a planner to explain the meter unit, actual usage and forecast assumptions.

Check comprehension without help; confirm units are never mixed.

03

Review the business case

Ask a reviewer to approve a plan or request changes with a reason.

Observe completion, evidence use, decision clarity and recovery from errors.

04

Use it with a keyboard

Complete navigation, meter inspection and approval without a mouse.

Check focus order, focus return, visible states and access to chart data.
