People & prework

Bring anonymized order lines, size curves, product revisions, released and held inventory, material availability, capacity plans and quality records. Interview production planners and supply representatives. Treat every unobserved assumption as a hypothesis.

Facilitator / experience designer: Lead the research, job framing and collaboration between business and technical teams.

Supply representative / user advocate: Bring clothing, footwear and equipment issue experiences, fit needs and delivery expectations.

Production planner / scheduler: Explain materials, line capacity, changeovers and feasible completion dates.

Quality / inventory owner: Distinguish released, allocated and held stock; verify product revision and lot evidence.

Operations / customer service lead: Own schedule decisions and communicate delivery exceptions through approved channels.

Agent builder / data lead: Design read-only lookups, source traceability, review states and outcome measurement.

Room & working agreements

Seven activity zones: supply needs, job stories, order-to-delivery map, agent responsibilities, recovery prototype, test scenarios and pilot plan. Use the same materials on a digital wall for remote participants.

  • Begin with the person and their circumstances.
  • Separate evidence, interpretation and ideas.
  • Write silently before discussion; make space for every voice.
  • Preserve dissent and assign a test to the disputed assumption.
  • Use consented, anonymized examples and minimum necessary data.

Missing evidence becomes an assigned research task. If the team cannot confirm capacity or quality status, retain the hold and escalate the exception. Shorten the prototype before removing the review step.

Forces, alternatives & success

trigger: A fictional order for 120 pairs of size 10 Regular boots has only 72 released pairs available. The remaining 48 pairs need a production plan before the Day 10 ship-by target.

alternatives: Reconcile spreadsheets, call the floor, chase quality documents and send revised dates by email.

push: An approaching ship-by target, fragmented records and a shortfall hidden by total inventory counts.

pull: One source-linked plan with the exact shortage, proposed production slot, quality evidence and accountable owner.

anxiety: A plan could rely on the wrong size, held stock or an unconfirmed completion date.

habit: Expedite the loudest request or assume a similar item will satisfy the order.

success: An approved, feasible plan for the unchanged order requirements, or a clearly owned exception when no plan works.

03 / RECORD THE DECISION

No recovery plan approved yet.

The agent assembles evidence. People approve the plan and own the next action.

What the agent checks
  1. Match the ordered item, size, revision and quantity.
  2. Separate released inventory from allocated and quality-held lots.
  3. Verify material, production and inspection capacity against the target date.
  4. Keep source timestamps and the exact reviewed plan together.
  5. Hold changed, rejected or unanswered plans for human action.

Before a live pilot: validate these proposed checks against the manufacturer’s actual processes, order terms and decision authorities.

The proposed user study and decision gates

Hypothesis

A source-linked recovery plan reduces reconciliation effort while preserving exact order requirements and human decision ownership.

Participants

Proposed formative study: 4 planners, 2 quality reviewers and 2 supply/customer-service representatives. Exploratory research, not a representative sample.

Tasks

  1. Resolve the 48-pair boot shortage without changing size or revision.
  2. Explain the stock, production and quality evidence behind the date.
  3. Reject or expire a review request and assign the next owner.
  4. Change an approved plan and verify that approval is cleared.
  5. Handle wrong sizes, held lots, stale records and late production.

Observation

Observe planning time, evidence comprehension, requirement errors and exception ownership. Compare with the existing manual workflow and record the exact approved version.

Advance, revise or stop

Proposed gate: all scripted requirement, hold and approval checks pass; at least 7 of 8 participants identify the shortfall, completion date and decision owner unaided. Any bypass of a quality hold or unapproved commitment requires redesign.

Agree decision authority and data freshness with the manufacturer before a limited pilot. The scripted demo does not establish production performance.

Learn → revise → test again

If the job is wrong, return to discovery. If the handoff fails, revise the blueprint. If the response misleads, fix the source, prompt or interface and rerun the case.
